1. At reception: what to say

English
“Before you see the doctor, could you share your family's health history? It takes two minutes on your phone: scan this code, write who in your family had which illnesses, and tick to share it with us. {{LANGNAME}} is fine. It's private and you can stop sharing any time.”

  • Best moments: when booking (add the link to the SMS or WhatsApp reminder), at check-in, and while waiting.

2. Common questions

Is it free?Yes, for patients.
Who sees my information?This clinic's care team, and only because you chose to share. Not insurers, not employers, never sold. You see a log of every access in the app.
Can I stop sharing?Yes, any time, in the app (Settings → Your clinics) or by asking us. Deleting the account erases everything.
Is this a diagnosis?No. It points out what may be worth discussing with the doctor, based on published screening guidelines.
Do I need the app?No. The link works in the phone's browser. The app adds reminders, documents and family members.
Can I add my parents' reports?Yes, in the app: photograph a prescription or lab report and choose whose it is.

3. For doctors and nurses: using the portal

  • Open {{PORTAL}} and sign in. The worklist lists patients who shared, highest priority first, with the care programs each may qualify for.
  • Open a patient's summary before the visit: action items, the guideline behind each, and a family tree.
  • When you refer someone (genetic counseling, lipid clinic, earlier screening), set the referral status. Partners update it as they schedule and complete, and the patient sees it in the app.
  • The referral funnel and Download pilot report show how many patients were identified, referred, scheduled and seen.

4. Do and don't

  • Let the patient (or a family member they choose) type and tick consent themselves.
  • Say clearly that it's optional and won't affect their care.
  • Treat action items as prompts for the doctor, not results.
  • Report anything that looks wrong to the pilot coordinator.
  • Don't fill it in on a patient's behalf without them, or tick consent for them.
  • Don't tell a patient they “have” a condition because of an action item.
  • Don't take photos of patients' records on staff phones.
  • Don't share portal logins; each staff member joins with the staff code.
